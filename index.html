Editing Program

While there are some programs out there, I'm recommending Affinity Studio by Canva. It's free editing software with no subscription, ads, etc. and works just as well as Photoshop.

I know Canva has a free mode but it won't let you export graphics with a transparent BG. A workaround is to use a colored background and a background remover site to clean it up for you, but sometimes it just doesn't work, so this is the alternative I'm offering.

If you're a Canva user, I believe it also has some features tied to Canva, like the AI generative part that PS has. I'm just unsure which ones since I don't use Canva.

If you're completely new to editing software, it might be a bit of a learning curve but there are a lot of tutorials on YT that you can learn from.

When I have the time, I'll make a step by step basic tutorial on how to make your own graphics with Affinity Studio + a downloadable template file you can play around with to make your own.

Layouting Text Content

When presenting your content, like in bot bios, it's best to prioritize what the user wants to know and put the rest of the "nice to know" near the end. You're free to rearrange it, but I usually go with this:

  1. Hook line to draw users in to the bot and/or followed by an overview of the premise
  2. Warnings
  3. Who is Char
  4. About User/User's Role
  5. Other important stuff
  6. Scenarios
  7. Image Gallery (I usually support these with captions on each image)
  8. World Lore/Supporting Info
  9. Yap section/Author's Notes

When presenting information, make it concise. Ensure everything is chunked down and not information overload/wall of text.

On lore-heavy bots, I add just enough information to not overwhelm the user, with a brief world lore/info they can explore further by clicking on the lorebook/lore site if they want to know more. For example, instead of "The Land of Fruits has 10 countries: the apple, the orange, the kiwi, etc..." just go with "The Land of Fruits is a dark fantasy world with 10 countries named after fruits."

In summary: curate the context in a way that's still authentic to who and what your bot is about, without making it way too intimidating for a user to interact with. Break up walls of text into paragraphs and present key information first, then "nice to know" content towards the bottom.

Models

What are models?
Models are programs trained on different things and built with pattern recognition based on the stuff they're trained on. Some are trained to be better at coding (HTML, CSS, JavaScript, etc.), problem solving, or content writing. Once they're fed information, they break it down, apply what they've been trained on, and put out a response appropriate to what was asked of them.

Not all models under the same name function the same. For example, Kimi 2.5 is good at content writing but not as good as Kimi 2.7 Code at coding a website.

I currently use Kimi, Deepseek, and GLM models at 0.6 temp. As of writing this, I like using Kimi Code 2.7 and DS 0813 in my RPs.

Trying out Custom Providers

As someone who lives in SE Asia, I refuse to spend more on an A.I hobby, so I try to get the most bang for my buck by finding platforms that offer a subscription with a lot of models included.

I've tried OpenRouter, Chutes, and as of writing this, I'm currently subbed on Nano-GPT.

If you're not a heavy RPer/user, it's better to go on a top-up/pay-as-you-go basis than to sub. And if you're loyal to one model, like Deepseek or GLM, it's always better to go directly to the source. Some, like Deepseek, offer cheaper prices during off-peak hours.

Tips to look out for when exploring a new provider

  1. Some platforms give you a free trial, try them out first before committing.
  2. Check for service stability and the number of models offered. If it's super cheap and offers models like Claude, get sussy. Chances are that place is gonna get nuked in the future or shut down as soon as they reach their quota of information extracted.
  3. While free is good, most of them extract and sell your info or do other shady stuff.

Bottomline: while most or all of them might be extracting your info and selling it to who knows what, just pick the ones least likely to leak your 10 inch mafia CEO RP to the Facebook or Instagram account tied to the email you used to register on their site (,; ⩌ ;,)
